Plant circadian biology

How plants keep time.

We investigate the molecular mechanisms that generate and coordinate circadian rhythms in plants—from chromatin and transcription to intercellular communication, growth and crop productivity.

About the lab

Time is part of plant biology.

Led by Prof. Paloma Mas at the Centre for Research in Agricultural Genomics (CRAG), The Clock Lab studies how plants measure time and use temporal information to synchronize development, metabolism and responses to the environment.

Research

From clocks to crops

We combine molecular genetics, functional genomics, cell biology and computational approaches in Arabidopsis and tomato.

01

Circadian mechanisms

Understanding the molecular networks that generate robust 24-hour rhythms and connect environmental signals with plant physiology.

02

Chromatin & transcription

Studying how chromatin state, transcription and genome regulation change across the day–night cycle.

03

Communication & growth

Exploring how clocks communicate across cells, tissues and organs to coordinate development and crop performance.
